# Marketing Thread — Kickoff Brief

Paste this into the marketing chat to start it with accurate context.

## What you are selling

**Corporate Academy Career Readiness Program** — a 12-week, 14-module professional-readiness programme sold to institutions, not to individual students. Buyers are career services directors, honors college deans, Greek life organisations and leadership nonprofits.

The product idea in one line: **the workbook reimagined as an interactive web app.** Students complete exercises, those answers build a personal profile, and the profile becomes a portfolio they keep.

The platform lives at **learn.corpacad.com**.

## The business model shapes the pitch

**Institutions buy it; they do not log in.** Reporting is *push* — Oscar generates a report and sends it. There is no customer portal, no institutional dashboard, no seat management. Do not imply otherwise in any material. It is a deliberate choice, and it is defensible: the institution gets a written report and a cover note rather than another login they will never use.

## What is real today — safe to claim

Every item below has been verified running in production.

- **14 modules across 3 pillars**, each with workbook content, exercises and a 4-question quiz. Content is the real curriculum, not placeholder.
- **5 live sessions** on alternating weeks, tracked as non-blocking milestones.
- **A living workbook.** Students answer structured exercises — identity anchors, rewrite drills, scorecards, STAR stories — and the answers persist to their profile.
- **CAPRI**, the Corporate Academy Professional Readiness Index: a 33-item instrument across 3 pillars, taken at Week 1 and Week 12, with a built-in correction for response-shift bias.
- **A portfolio document** the student generates at completion, curates themselves, and keeps as a PDF — it works without a Corporate Academy account.
- **Institutional reporting**: participation, completion, workbook depth, quiz performance, attendance, students needing attention with reasons, and change since the last report. Generated on a schedule.
- **Certification** on completing all 14 modules and the Week 12 assessment.

## What is NOT ready — do not claim it

- **Module videos are in production.** The platform supports them; the recordings are not uploaded yet. If a prospect asks, "the video library is being finalised" is true. **Do not show a live demo of a module's Watch section** until the videos are in.
- **No cohort has completed the programme.** There are no outcome results, no testimonials, no case studies, and no "students improved X%" claim available. The instrument exists; the evidence does not yet.
- **No institution-facing login.** Covered above.

## How to talk about CAPRI — this one matters

CAPRI measures **self-assessed readiness**, before and after. That is a real and meaningful outcome, and it is **not** the same as measured capability.

- Say: *"students rate their own professional readiness across three pillars at Week 1 and Week 12, and we report the change."*
- Do **not** say: *"readiness improved 23%"* as though it were an objective test result, or imply a third party assessed the student.

The platform already enforces this internally: the student's own portfolio shows direction of change with no numbers, precisely so a recruiter cannot misread a self-rating as a grade. Marketing should hold the same line. An institution that discovers the index is self-reported, having been told something stronger, is a lost renewal.

Two further honesty points worth knowing:
- **Live-session attendance is currently self-reported by students** (an admin confirmation path exists). Reports label it as such. Do not present attendance as verified.
- Small cohorts may produce **no** outcome figures at all — cohort-level claims are withheld below 8 paired responses, deliberately, to avoid over-claiming on thin data.

## The strongest things to lead with

1. **Students leave with an artifact**, not a certificate of attendance. The portfolio is the differentiator, and most competing programmes have nothing equivalent.
2. **The institution gets a real report** without administering anything — no login, no training, no staff time.
3. **Completion means something.** A module counts as complete only when every workbook exercise is answered *and* the quiz is passed at 75% or above. That standard is enforced server-side and is unusually strict for this category.
4. **Pre/post measurement is built in**, with an explicit correction for the bias that normally inflates self-assessment gains.

## First tasks worth doing

1. **The one-page institution-facing overview** — what the programme is, what the student does, what the institution receives, what it costs.
2. **The honest outcome language**, written once and reused, so nobody improvises a percentage in an email.
3. **The Squarespace login entry point** — where students land, what the button says, what a lost student does.
4. **The first-cohort story**, planned in advance: what gets measured, what gets written up, and what consent is needed before any student's work appears in marketing.

`docs/institutional-summary-prompt.md` in the repo already contains a template for the written summary that accompanies each institutional report. Reuse its voice: direct, plain, no vendor enthusiasm.

## Hard rule

**Student workbook answers are private.** They are visible inside a cohort only when the student marks them public, and they never appear in institutional reports — reports carry counts only. No student's written work may be used in marketing without explicit, specific, written consent from that student. The product makes this promise to students on screen; marketing has to keep it.
